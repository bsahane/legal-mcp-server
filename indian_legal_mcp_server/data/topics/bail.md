# Bail in India

## Overview

Bail is the release of an accused person from custody on furnishing a bond or surety. It balances the presumption of innocence against the need to secure the accused's presence at trial. Under BNSS 2023, bail provisions are in Chapter XXXIV (ss. 478-493). The law distinguishes between bailable and non-bailable offences.

## Types of Bail

### Regular Bail (ss. 478-479 BNSS)
- s. 478: Police shall release on bail if offence is bailable
- s. 479: Magistrate shall release on bail if offence is bailable
- Applies to bailable offences (shown as bailable in First Schedule)
- Right to bail is absolute for bailable offences

### Bail in Non-Bailable Offences (s. 483 BNSS)
- Discretionary, not a right
- Court considers: nature of offence, evidence, likelihood of tampering, criminal antecedents
- Factors for grant: flight risk, witness tampering, evidence tampering, public order
- Bail conditions may include: surrender of passport, regular reporting, surety

### Anticipatory Bail (s. 482 BNSS)
- Pre-arrest bail — applied BEFORE arrest
- Available from High Court or Court of Session
- Person must show reasonable apprehension of arrest for non-bailable offence
- Court may impose conditions: cooperate with investigation, not leave jurisdiction

### Default Bail (s. 187 BNSS)
- If investigation not completed within prescribed period:
  - 60 days for offences punishable up to 10 years
  - 90 days for offences punishable with death/life/10+ years
- Accused becomes entitled to bail on expiry
- Must be released if willing to furnish bail

### Bail Pending Appeal (s. 280 BNSS)
- Appellate Court may suspend sentence and grant bail pending appeal
- Court considers: merits of appeal, sentence length, likelihood of absconding

## Comparison: Bailable vs Non-Bailable

| Feature | Bailable | Non-Bailable |
|---|---|---|
| Right to bail | Absolute | Discretionary |
| Arrest procedure | May be arrested without warrant if cognizable | Usually arrested |
| Judicial discretion | Minimal | Wide |
| Default period | N/A | 60/90 days |

## Grounds for Cancellation of Bail (s. 189 BNSS)
- Breach of bail conditions
- Tampering with evidence
- Threatening witnesses
- Committing another offence while on bail
- Flight risk / non-appearance in Court

## Key Procedural Steps

1. **Arrest** → produced before Magistrate within 24 hours
2. **Bail application** → filed before Magistrate/Sessions Court/High Court
3. **Notice to Public Prosecutor** → PP may oppose on valid grounds
4. **Order** → Court passes speaking order granting/refusing bail
5. **Bond execution** → accused executes bond with or without sureties
6. **Release** → jail authorities release on receipt of release order

## Constitutional Backing
- Art. 21: Right to life and liberty — bail is the rule, jail is the exception
- Art. 22: Right against arbitrary arrest and detention

## Relevant Sections
- **BNSS s. 478-482**: Types of bail (regular, anticipatory, default)
- **BNSS s. 483**: Factors for grant of bail in non-bailable offences
- **BNSS s. 187**: Default bail on expiry of investigation period

- **BNSS s. 280**: Bail pending appeal
- **BNS relevant**: Various bail-worthy offences under BNS

## Common Situations

### False 498A / Dowry Case
- Anticipatory bail (s. 482 BNSS) commonly sought
- Court may grant with conditions: cooperate with investigation, not threaten witnesses

### NDPS / POCSO Cases
- Stringent conditions — bail rarely granted in commercial quantities
- Twin conditions under NDPS s. 37 apply

### Economic Offences
- Larger conspiracies attract stricter bail scrutiny
- Court may order attachment of property

## Limitations
- Bail is not grantable for offences punishable with death or life imprisonment without strong grounds
- Repeat offenders face presumption against bail
- Victim's right to be heard before bail cancellation

## Exceptions
- Offences under special acts (NDPS, PMLA, UAPA) have stricter bail provisions
- S. 45 PMLA: twin conditions for bail
- S. 37 NDPS: public prosecutor must be heard
- S. 43D(5) UAPA: bail not to be granted if accusation appears prima facie true
