# Divorce under Hindu Marriage Act, 1955

## Overview

The Hindu Marriage Act, 1955 governs divorce for Hindus, Buddhists, Jains, and Sikhs. It provides for both contested divorce (on fault grounds under s. 13) and mutual consent divorce (under s. 13B). The Act applies to any person who is Hindu by religion, including Virashaiva, Lingayat, or follower of Brahmo, Prarthana, or Arya Samaj.

## Grounds for Divorce (s. 13 HMA) — Contested Divorce

A spouse may petition for divorce on any of these grounds:

| Ground | Description | Key Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Adultery | Voluntary sexual intercourse outside marriage | Proof of relationship |
| Cruelty | Physical or mental cruelty endangering life or health | Medical records, FIRs, messages |
| Desertion | Willful abandonment for 2+ years (continuous) | Separate residence evidence |
| Conversion | Ceased to be Hindu by conversion | Conversion certificate |
| Unsound mind | Incurable mental disorder for 3+ years | Medical reports |
| Leprosy | Incurable form for 3+ years | Medical certificate |
| Venereal disease | Communicable form | Medical evidence |
| Renunciation | Entered religious order | Proof of sannyasa |
| Presumed death | Not heard alive for 7+ years | Missing person report |
| No resumption | After decree of judicial separation, 1+ year no cohabitation | Judicial separation decree |

### Wife-only grounds (s. 13(2)):
- Pre-Act polygamy by husband
- Rape, sodomy, bestiality by husband
- Maintenance order under s. 125 CrPC / DV Act not complied with
- Option of puberty exercised (marriage before 15)

## Mutual Consent Divorce (s. 13B HMA)

1. Both spouses jointly petition
2. Living separately for 1+ year
3. Unable to live together
4. Mutual agreement on terms
5. 6-month cooling period (may be waived by SC/HC)
6. After 6-18 months, second motion for dissolution

## Procedure

### Contested Divorce
1. **Petition** filed in District Court
2. **Service** on respondent
3. **Written statement** by respondent
4. **Issues** framed by court
5. **Evidence** by both parties
6. **Arguments**
7. **Judgment**

### Mutual Consent Divorce
1. **First motion** — joint petition with settlement terms
2. **Cooling period** — 6 months (may be waived)
3. **Second motion** — after minimum period, parties confirm consent
4. **Decree** — court grants divorce

## Limitation
- No limitation period for filing divorce petition
- For restitution of conjugal rights: 1 year from date of cause of action
- For judicial separation: no limitation

## Alternative Relief

| Relief | Provision | When Used |
|---|---|---|
| Restitution of Conjugal Rights | s. 9 HMA | Spouse withdrawn from society without reasonable excuse |
| Judicial Separation | s. 10 HMA | Grounds similar to divorce but marriage continues |
| Divorce | s. 13 HMA | Complete dissolution of marriage |
| Void / Voidable Marriage | ss. 11-12 HMA | Bigamy, prohibited degrees, impotence |

## Key Judgments

| Case | Principle |
|---|---|
| S. Saravanan v. M. Jayanthi (2024) | irretrievable breakdown not statutory ground but SC can dissolve marriage |
| Shilpa Sailesh v. Varun Sreenivasan (2023) | Mutual consent waiver of 6-month period |
| Naveen Kohli v. Neelu Kohli (2006) | irretrievable breakdown recognized as ground |
| Samar Ghosh v. Jaya Ghosh (2007) | Cruelty — comprehensive guidelines |
| R. Durga Prasad v. U. Sandhya (2020) | Mental cruelty — objective standard |

## Maintenance in Divorce Proceedings

- **Pendente lite maintenance** (s. 24 HMA): during proceedings
- **Permanent alimony** (s. 25 HMA): lump sum or periodic after divorce
- **Child custody** (s. 26 HMA): welfare of child is paramount
- Court considers: income, status, conduct, duration of marriage

## Common Issues
- **Irretrievable breakdown**: Not a statutory ground but SC may grant divorce under Art. 142
- **Cruelty**: Most commonly litigated ground — includes verbal abuse, false allegations, sexual incompatibility
- **Adultery**: Proving direct evidence difficult — circumstantial evidence suffices
- **Divorce by mutual consent**: Most efficient route if both agree
