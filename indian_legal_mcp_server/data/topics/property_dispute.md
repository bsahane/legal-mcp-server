# Property Disputes in India

## Overview

Property disputes are among the most litigated matters in Indian civil courts. They span ownership claims, inheritance, boundary disputes, landlord-tenant conflicts, and title defects. The Limitation Act 1963 provides 12 years for recovery of immovable property (Schedule I, Art. 64-67).

## Types of Property Disputes

### Title / Ownership Disputes
- Disputed ownership based on sale deeds, inheritance, or adverse possession
- Court may declare title and order possession
- Limitation: 12 years from date of dispossession

### Partition Disputes
- Co-owners seeking division of joint family property
- Governed by Hindu Succession Act (coparcenary) or general law
- No limitation for right to claim partition while joint tenancy exists

### Boundary / Easement Disputes
- Disputes over boundary lines, right of way, drainage
- Easement by prescription requires 20 years of use (Limitation Act s. 25)
- Civil suit for injunction or declaration

### Landlord-Tenant Disputes
- Eviction, rent arrears, tenancy rights
- Governed by state-specific Rent Control Acts
- BNS s. 333 (IPC s. 441) criminal trespass applies if possession is illegal

### Adverse Possession
- Hostile, continuous, exclusive possession for 12 years
- Owner's title is extinguished under Limitation Act s. 27
- Burden of proof on the person claiming adverse possession

## Relevant Sections

- **BNS ss. 329-333**: Criminal trespass, house-trespass, lurking house-trespass
- **BNS s. 337**: Mischief (damage to property)
- **BNS ss. 348-349**: Forgery of documents, title deeds
- **Limitation Act s. 25**: Easement by prescription (20 years)
- **Limitation Act s. 27**: Extinguishment of title (12 years adverse possession)
- **Limitation Act Art. 64-67**: 12 years for immovable property
- **TPA ss. 54-55**: Sale of immovable property, seller's/buyer's duties
- **CPC ss. 9, 100**: Civil court jurisdiction, second appeal

## Procedural Steps

1. **Cause of Action** → Dispute arises (refusal to vacate, denial of title, encroachment)
2. **Legal Notice** → Send notice to opposing party (not mandatory but recommended)
3. **Suit Filing** → File in civil court ( jurisdictional value determines court level)
4. **Written Statement** → Defendant's response within 30 days
5. **Issues Framing** → Court frames issues for trial
6. **Evidence** → Plaintiff's evidence, then defendant's (oral + documentary)
7. **Arguments** → Final hearing and submissions
8. **Judgment** → Court decides rights and entitlements
9. **Execution** → Decree-holder applies for execution if needed

## Common Situations

### Builder Delayed Possession
- Consumer complaint (CPA 2019) or civil suit for possession
- RERA complaint for registered projects

### Inheritance / Succession Dispute
- Succession certificate or probate (if Will exists)
- Partition suit if no Will
- Hindu Succession Act governs intestate succession for Hindus

### Encroachment on Land
- Civil suit for permanent injunction
- If government land: public interest litigation or revenue authority complaint
- Criminal remedy: BNS s. 329 (trespass)

## Limitations

- 12-year limitation for recovery of immovable property (strictly enforced)
- No limitation applies to government suits (s. 32)
- 3-year limitation for recovery of movable property
- 1-year limitation for torts affecting property
