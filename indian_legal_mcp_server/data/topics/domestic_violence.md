# Domestic Violence — Protection of Women from Domestic Violence Act, 2005

## Overview

The Protection of Women from Domestic Violence Act, 2005 (DV Act) is a civil law that provides protection to women in domestic relationships. Unlike criminal law (which punishes after the fact), the DV Act focuses on prevention, protection, and immediate relief. It covers physical, emotional, sexual, verbal, and economic abuse.

## Key Definitions

### Domestic Violence (s. 3 DV Act)
Includes:
- **Physical abuse**: Actual or threatened bodily harm
- **Sexual abuse**: Forced sexual conduct, marital rape context
- **Verbal/emotional abuse**: Insults, ridicule, humiliation, repeated threats
- **Economic abuse**: Deprivation of financial resources, assets, stridhan, prohibition from employment

### Domestic Relationship (s. 2(f))
- Relationship by marriage, blood, or adoption
- Includes: wives, mothers, sisters, daughters, live-in partners (Indra Sarma v. VKV Nair, 2013)
- **Live-in relationship** in the nature of marriage qualifies

### Shared Household (s. 2(s))
- House where aggrieved person lived or presently lives
- Includes: matrimonial home, parental home, rented premises
- Right to reside in shared household (s. 17)

## Who Can File?
- Any woman in a domestic relationship
- Complaint can be filed by the aggrieved person or any person on her behalf
- Against: husband, male partner, male relative, or their relatives

## Procedure

### Step 1: Filing Complaint
- Before: Judicial Magistrate (First Class), Protection Officer, Police, Service Provider
- Format: simple application — no court fee required
- Can be filed at: place of residence, place of violence, or where respondent resides

### Step 2: Application to Magistrate (s. 12)
- Magistrate fixes first hearing within 3 days
- Must dispose of application within 60 days from first hearing

### Step 3: Orders by Magistrate

| Order Type | Provision | What It Does |
|---|---|---|
| Protection Order | s. 18 | Prohibits respondent from committing DV, contacting, entering workplace |
| Residence Order | s. 19 | Right to stay in shared household, alternate accommodation |
| Monetary Relief | s. 20 | Compensation for loss, medical expenses, economic abuse |
| Custody Order | s. 21 | Temporary custody of child |
| Compensation Order | s. 22 | Damages for mental torture and emotional distress |

### Step 4: Enforcement
- Breach of protection order is a criminal offence (s. 31)
- Punishment: imprisonment up to 1 year, fine up to ₹20,000, or both
- Cognizable and non-bailable

## Role of Service Providers
- **Protection Officer (s. 8)**: Government-appointed, assists with filing, shelter, medical aid
- **Service Provider (s. 9)**: NGOs registered under the Act
- **Medical facilities**: Must provide treatment
- **Shelter homes**: Must provide shelter

## Protection Officers (s. 8 DV Act)
- Every district has Protection Officers
- Duties: assist in filing, maintain records, accompany to court, coordinate with service providers

## Criminal Law Overlap (BNS)

Under BNS 2023:
- s. 85: Dowry death (causing death within 7 years of marriage)
- s. 86: Cruelty by husband or relative (physical/mental)
- s. 87: Demand for dowry

Filing under DV Act does NOT bar criminal complaint under BNS.

## Key Judgments

| Case | Principle |
|---|---|
| Indra Sarma v. VKV Nair (2013) | Live-in relationship in nature of marriage covered |
| V.D. Bhanot v. Savita Bhanot (2012) | Shared household includes parents' property |
| S.R. Batra v. Taruna Batra (2007) | Shared household limited to property owned/rented by family |
| Kunapareddy v. Kunapareddy (2008) | Definition of domestic violence is broad |
| Sandhya Wankhede v. State of Maharashtra (2023) | Speedy disposal — time limits strictly enforced |

## Immediate Steps for Victim

1. **Call 112** or local police for immediate danger
2. **Visit nearest Protection Officer** or District Legal Services Authority
3. **File application before Magistrate** (no lawyer needed)
4. **Collect evidence**: medical reports, photos, messages, call logs
5. **Seek shelter** if needed (short-stay homes available)

## Practical Tips
- Maintain diary of incidents with dates, times, and witnesses
- Preserve all medical records of injuries
- Keep proof of financial dependence (bank statements, salary slips)
- Note that DV Act proceedings are **civil** but breach is **criminal**
