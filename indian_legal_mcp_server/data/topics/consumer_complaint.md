# Consumer Complaints in India

## Overview

Consumer protection in India is governed by the Consumer Protection Act, 2019 (CPA 2019), which replaced the 1986 Act. It establishes a three-tier adjudicatory system: District Commission, State Commission, and National Commission. A consumer is any person who buys goods or hires services for consideration.

## Who is a Consumer

- Buys goods for personal use (not for resale/commercial purpose)
- Hires services for consideration
- Includes beneficiaries of services (e.g., hotel guest, patient)
- Does NOT include: persons buying for commercial purpose, persons availing services under free scheme

## Grounds for Complaint

### Defective Goods
- Manufacturing defect, quality issues, expiration, safety hazard
- Spurious / counterfeit goods
- Non-conformity with specifications

### Deficiency in Services
- Negligence, delay, substandard work
- Professional negligence (medical, legal, architectural)
- Banking / insurance / telecom service deficiencies

### Unfair Trade Practices
- False/misleading advertisements
- Bait-and-switch tactics
- Hoarding, manipulation of prices
- Free gift offers with hidden conditions

### Unfair Contracts
- One-sided terms favouring seller/service provider
- Excessive cancellation charges
- Unilateral price escalation clauses

## Three-Tier Adjudicatory System

| Forum | Pecuniary Jurisdiction | Appeal To |
|---|---|---|
| District Commission | Up to ₹1 crore | State Commission |
| State Commission | ₹1 crore to ₹10 crore | National Commission |
| National Commission | Above ₹10 crore | Supreme Court |

## Procedure

1. **Cause of Action** → Defect/deficiency arises
2. **Legal Notice** → Send notice to opposite party (optional but recommended)
3. **File Complaint** → Before appropriate commission within 2 years
4. **Admission** → Commission admits if complaint discloses cause of action
5. **Notice to Opposite Party** → 30 days to respond
6. **Evidence** → Affidavit evidence (CPA 2019 s. 38)
7. **Hearing** → Summary proceedings (not full civil trial)
8. **Order** → Commission passes reasoned order
9. **Compliance** → Order must be complied with within specified period
10. **Appeal** → 30 days from order to appellate forum

## Limitation

- 2 years from date of cause of action (CPA 2019 s. 69)
- Delay can be condoned if sufficient cause shown
- Limitation does NOT apply to continuing causes of action

## Relief Available

- Removal of defect / replacement of goods
- Refund of price / charges
- Compensation for loss or injury (including mental agony)
- Punitive damages for egregious conduct
- Cost of litigation (up to 35% of awarded amount)
- Cease and desist orders against unfair trade practices

## Relevant Provisions

- **CPA 2019 s. 2(7)**: Definition of consumer
- **CPA 2019 s. 2(11)**: Definition of defect
- **CPA 2019 s. 2(35)**: Unfair trade practice
- **CPA 2019 s. 49**: Product liability
- **CPA 2019 s. 69**: Limitation period (2 years)
- **CPA 2019 s. 72-74**: Penalties for non-compliance
- **BNS s. 316**: Cheating by personation (if fraud involved)
- **NI Act s. 138**: Cheque bounce (if payment by cheque)

## Common Situations

### Defective Mobile Phone / Electronics
- File complaint before District Commission
- Seek replacement or refund with compensation

### Delayed Possession of Flat / Plot
- Complaint under CPA 2019 for deficiency in service
- Can also approach RERA for registered projects

### Medical Negligence
- Consumer complaint against hospital/doctor
- Supreme Court: medical services are covered under CPA

### Insurance Claim Denied
- Complaint if claim wrongfully rejected or delayed
- Deficiency in service — consumer forum jurisdiction

### E-Commerce Issues
- Delayed delivery, wrong product, poor quality
- E-commerce platforms are liable as service providers

## Exceptions

- Free services not covered (must be for consideration)
- Commercial purpose purchases not covered
- Professional services covered only for deficiency, not for poor outcomes
