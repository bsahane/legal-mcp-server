# Maintenance — Maintenance of Wives, Children, and Parents

## Overview

Maintenance is financial support that a person is legally obliged to provide to their spouse, children, or parents. In India, maintenance is governed by multiple statutes depending on the personal law and circumstances. The BNSS 2023 (ss. 144-158) provides a secular, summary remedy available to all regardless of religion.

## Maintenance under BNSS 2023 (ss. 144-158)

This is the most commonly invoked maintenance provision. It is a summary criminal proceeding, available to wives, children, and parents.

### Who Can Claim (s. 144 BNSS)

| Claimant | Condition |
|---|---|
| Wife | Unable to maintain herself |
| Children (minor) | Unable to maintain themselves |
| Children (major) | Physical or mental disability |
| Parents | Unable to maintain themselves |

### Quantum of Maintenance (s. 145 BNSS)
- Maximum: ₹5,000 per month per person (or higher as per state amendments)
- Amount determined by: income of respondent, needs of claimant, standard of living
- Court uses its discretion to fix a reasonable amount

### Interim Maintenance (s. 146 BNSS)
- From date of application, not from date of order
- 60% of final maintenance may be ordered as interim
- Court can order payment from the date of application

### Alteration of Maintenance (s. 148 BNSS)
- Either party may apply for variation
- Grounds: change in circumstances, income, cost of living
- Order can be increased or decreased

### Enforcement (s. 147 BNSS)
- If default in payment: Magistrate may issue warrant for levy
- Distress warrant against movable property
- Imprisonment up to 1 month for default (does not discharge liability)

### Procedure
1. **Application** filed before JMFC (Judicial Magistrate First Class)
2. **Notice** to respondent (30 days for reply)
3. **Evidence** by both parties
4. **Interim maintenance** ordered during proceedings
5. **Final order** within 60 days of first hearing

## Maintenance under HMA 1955

### s. 24 HMA — Maintenance Pendente Lite
- During divorce/judicial separation proceedings
- Spouse unable to maintain themselves
- Both husband and wife can claim
- Quantum: reasonable and proportionate to income

### s. 25 HMA — Permanent Alimony
- After divorce decree
- One-time lump sum or periodic payments
- Factors: income, property, conduct, duration of marriage
- Can be modified on change of circumstances

## Maintenance under DV Act 2005
- s. 20: Monetary relief for domestic violence victims
- s. 23: Interim orders
- Similar factors but broader scope (includes economic abuse)

## Key Provisions Comparison

| Statute | Maximum | Who Can Get | Nature |
|---|---|---|---|
| BNSS s. 144 | ₹5,000 / month (subject to state amendments) | Wife, children, parents | Summary criminal |
| HMA s. 24 | No limit | Spouse during proceedings | Civil |
| HMA s. 25 | No limit | Spouse after divorce | Civil |

## Differences from Personal Laws

| Aspect | BNSS (Secular) | Muslim Personal Law | HMA |
|---|---|---|---|
| Applicability | All persons | Muslims only | Hindus only |
| Maximum amount | Capped (₹5,000) | Unlimited (via agreement/court) | Unlimited |
| Procedure | Summary (Magistrate) | Civil suit / Family Court | Civil suit / Family Court |
| Enforcement | Distress warrant + imprisonment | Civil execution | Civil execution |

## Key Judgments

| Case | Principle |
|---|---|
| Danial Latifi v. Union of India (2001) | Muslim women entitled to maintenance under s. 125 CrPC despite Muslim personal law |
| Shamima Farooqui v. Shahid Khan (2015) | Maintenance from date of application, not order |
| Rajnesh v. Neha (2020) | Comprehensive guidelines on maintenance across multiple statutes |
| Badshah v. Urmila Badshah Godse (2013) | Purposive interpretation — wife includes divorced wife |
| Manish Jain v. Akanksha Jain (2019) | Permanent alimony calculated considering standard of living |

## Common Defences

| Defence | Validity |
|---|---|
| "Wife is earning" | Must prove income sufficient for basic needs |
| "Wife left voluntarily" | No defence if unable to maintain herself |
| "Wife refused to live with husband" | Valid only if no justifiable cause for leaving |
| "Child is major" | Valid unless child has disability |
| "Parents have property" | Not a defence if unable to maintain themselves |

## Practical Tips for Claimant
- File before JMFC where you reside (easy jurisdiction)
- No court fee required in criminal proceedings
- Collect: income proof of respondent, your expense statement, medical records
- Interim maintenance available within weeks of filing

## Practical Tips for Respondent
- File reply with income proof (salary slips, IT returns)
- Disclose all assets (non-disclosure can be adverse)
- Pay interim maintenance when ordered — default leads to arrest
- Variation petition if circumstances change

## Overlapping Remedies
- DV Act s. 20: monetary relief (can be higher than BNSS)
- HMA s. 24/25: for Hindus
- Muslim Women (Protection of Rights on Divorce) Act, 1986
- Special Marriage Act, 1954 s. 36/37: for inter-faith marriages
