# Cheque Bounce — Negotiable Instruments Act, 1881 (s. 138)

## Overview

Section 138 of the NI Act makes dishonour of a cheque a criminal offence. It is India's highest-volume criminal litigation (over 7 crore cases pending, ~20% of all pending court cases). The provision aims to maintain the credibility of cheques as a payment instrument.

## Essential Ingredients (s. 138 NI Act)

ALL six conditions must be satisfied:

1. **Cheque drawn** on a bank account maintained by the drawer
2. **Cheque issued** for discharge of a legally enforceable debt or liability
3. **Cheque presented** to the bank within 3 months (or validity period) of issue
4. **Cheque returned** unpaid by the bank (insufficient funds / exceeds arrangement)
5. **Legal notice** served within 30 days of receiving the bank's dishonour memo
6. **No payment** made within 15 days of receiving the notice

## Procedure

### Step 1: Cheque Dishonour
- Bank issues dishonour memo with reason code (e.g., "insufficient funds", "account closed")

### Step 2: Legal Notice (within 30 days)
- Send via registered post / speed post / courier
- Demand payment within 15 days
- Address: branch where account is maintained
- Preserve: postal receipt, tracking proof, acknowledgment

### Step 3: Filing Complaint (within 30 days of notice expiry)
- File before Magistrate (JMFC) having jurisdiction
- Jurisdiction: multiple options after Dashrath Rupsingh Rathod amendment
  - Branch where cheque was presented
  - Branch of drawee's bank
  - Court with territorial jurisdiction over payee's location

### Step 4: Trial
- Summons-case procedure (BNSS Ch. XXI)
- Plea of accused recorded
- Evidence by complainant (affidavit evidence permitted)
- Evidence by defence
- Judgment

## Punishment

- Imprisonment up to 2 years
- Fine up to twice the cheque amount
- Both imprisonment and fine
- Compensation can be ordered to the complainant

## Defences Available

- **No legally enforceable debt**: Cheque issued as gift, advance, security
- **Cheque not signed** by accused
- **Notice not properly served**
- **Time limits not complied with** (30+15+30 day windows)
- **Account already closed** — heightens culpability
- **Stop payment** — not a defence if debt was legally enforceable
- **Signature disputed** — rarely succeeds (presumption under s. 20 Negotiable Instruments Act)
- **Cheque given as security** — recent SC decisions protect genuine security arrangements

## Limitation Period

- Complaint must be filed within 1 month of cause of action (notice expiry + 15 days)
- No extension possible
- Courts may condone delay up to 30 more days for sufficient cause

## Compoundable Offence (s. 147)
- Matter can be settled between parties at any stage
- On settlement, accused is acquitted
- Settlement amount usually = cheque amount + costs

## Practical Tips for Complainant
- Keep certified copy of dishonour memo
- Send notice within 30 days (mandatory)
- File within 30 days of notice expiry
- Preserve all bank statements, ledger, contract
- Affidavit evidence can avoid oral examination

## Practical Tips for Accused
- Respond to notice within 15 days
- Preserve evidence of payment or settlement
- Consider compounding early to avoid litigation costs
- File for discharge if ingredients not met
