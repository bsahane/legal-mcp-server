# Arrest and FIR in India

## Overview

The law of arrest and First Information Report (FIR) is governed by the Bharatiya Nagarik Suraksha Sanhita 2023 (BNSS), which replaced the CrPC 1973. An FIR is the first document recording cognizable information about an offence. Arrest is the deprivation of personal liberty, governed by strict procedural safeguards under BNSS and Constitution Art. 20-22.

## FIR (First Information Report)

### What is an FIR
- First information given to police about a cognizable offence
- Governed by BNSS ss. 173-178
- Must be recorded in writing (zero FIR allowed if wrong jurisdiction)
- Copy must be given free of cost to informant

### Types of FIR
- **Cognizable FIR** (BNSS s. 173): Police can investigate without court order
- **Zero FIR** (BNSS s. 173): Filed for wrong jurisdiction, then transferred
- **Non-Cognizable Complaint** (BNSS s. 174): Requires Magistrate order for investigation

### Rights of the Informant
- Right to have FIR recorded (BNSS s. 173(1))
- Right to free copy (BNSS s. 173(3))
- Right to have FIR registered if cognizable offence is disclosed
- If police refuse to register: approach SP or Magistrate under BNSS s. 173(4) or s. 175

### Contents of an FIR
- Date, time, place of occurrence
- Full description of the incident
- Names and descriptions of accused (if known)
- Names of witnesses
- Cause of death/injury (for unnatural death cases)
- Signature/thumb impression of informant

## Arrest

### Grounds for Arrest

- **Cognizable offence** (BNSS s. 35): Police may arrest without warrant
- **Non-cognizable offence** (BNSS s. 36): Requires Magistrate's warrant
- **Preventive arrest** (BNSS s. 125-132): For security/peace
- **Arrest under warrant** (BNSS ss. 66-76): Issued by court

### Arrest Procedure (BNSS ss. 43-65)

1. Notification of grounds (s. 52): Person arrested must be informed of grounds
2. Production before Magistrate (s. 53): Within 24 hours (excl. travel time)
3. Medical examination (s. 54): Right to medical examination
4. Right to counsel (s. 55): Right to consult legal practitioner
5. Search and seizure (ss. 56-65): Proper search procedure
6. Arrest memo (ss. 43(3)): Must be prepared and witnessed

### Rights Upon Arrest

| Right | Source |
|---|---|
| To know grounds of arrest | BNSS s. 52, Art. 22(1) |
| To be produced within 24 hours | BNSS s. 53, Art. 22(2) |
| To consult lawyer | BNSS s. 55, Art. 22(1) |
| To inform family/friend | BNSS s. 52(2) |
| To medical examination | BNSS s. 54 |
| Not to be handcuffed arbitrarily | Police manual guidelines |
| To apply for bail | BNSS ss. 478-493 |
| To remain silent | Art. 20(3) |

### Types of Arrest
- **Arrest by police** (warrant/warrantless)
- **Arrest by Magistrate** (BNSS s. 70)
- **Arrest by private person** (BNSS s. 44)
- **Arrest for non-bailable offence** (BNSS s. 35(1)(b))
- **Arrest in bailable offence** (BNSS s. 35(1)(a))

## Remand (BNSS ss. 86-88, 187)

- **Police Custody**: Max 15 days (from date of production)
- **Judicial Custody**: 60/90 days depending on offence
- Default bail (s. 187): If investigation not completed within period

## Key Distinctions

| Feature | Cognizable | Non-Cognizable |
|---|---|---|
| Arrest without warrant | Allowed | Not allowed |
| Police investigation | Without court order | Requires Magistrate order |
| Examples | Murder, theft, rape | Defamation, public nuisance |

## Common Situations

### Brother Arrested Without FIR
- Police must register FIR or provide written reason for refusal
- Arrest without FIR is illegal if no cognizable offence disclosed
- File habeas corpus petition under Art. 226

### Landlord-Lodger Dispute → False FIR
- File anticipatory bail (BNSS s. 482)
- Apply for quashing of FIR under Art. 226 / BNSS s. 528

## Landmark Cases

- **Lalita Kumari vs. State of UP (2014)**: Police MUST register FIR if cognizable offence disclosed
- **D.K. Basu vs. State of WB (1997)**: Arrest procedure guidelines — protection against custodial violence
- **Joginder Kumar vs. State of UP (1994)**: Arrest not routine — must be justified
- **Arnesh Kumar vs. State of Bihar (2014)**: No automatic arrest for offences up to 7 years

## Exceptions

- Preventive detention can exceed 24 hours (but requires review board)
- Enemy aliens have limited Art. 22 protection
- Certain special acts (NDPS, PMLA, UAPA) have modified arrest provisions
